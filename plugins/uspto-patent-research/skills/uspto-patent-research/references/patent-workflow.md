# Patent research and drafting workflow

## Intake facts

Capture inventors and their contributions, applicant/assignee, earliest conception and reduction-to-practice evidence, prior filings, public disclosures, offers for sale, deployments, publications, and foreign-filing plans.

## Search matrix

For every invention element, search:

- exact terminology and functional synonyms;
- older terminology used before the current product category existed;
- likely CPC/IPC classes;
- adjacent industries solving the same technical problem;
- closest assignees and their backward/forward citations;
- patents, published applications, standards, papers, manuals, products, videos, repositories, and archived pages.

## Evidence table

Record: reference; jurisdiction/publication; priority/publication dates; status; technical disclosure; mapped claim elements; missing elements; combination rationale; source; confidence; researcher notes.

## Application package

Draft, in order: title; cross-reference; field; background; summary; drawing list; detailed description; alternatives; examples; claims; abstract. Keep the background restrained and avoid unnecessary admissions about what is prior art.

For computer-implemented inventions, describe concrete data structures, transformations, state transitions, confidence thresholds, source provenance, exception handling, access controls, reversible actions, failure behavior, and system boundaries. Include enough variants to support narrow fallback claims.

## Review gates

- Every claimed concept has written-description support.
- Terminology is consistent across figures, description, and claims.
- Method steps have corresponding system and non-transitory-medium support.
- Optional features are not accidentally described as mandatory.
- Purchase is distinguished from consumption.
- Inventorship is based on contribution to claimed subject matter.
- Known closest art is candidly mapped without making legal conclusions.
- Filing status language is accurate.
